## Key Findings

### 1. T3 usage is minimal in BudgetFlow policies

`budgetflow_task_level` used T3 2.9% of turns, `budgetflow_segment` 5.3%. The t3x3 catalog's cost_ratio=4.80 produces frontier scores that discourage T3 escalation. Combined with weak catalog progress priors (postmortem Gap A: deltas 0.01-0.03), BudgetFlow almost never finds T3 justified.

`bare_t3_baseline` confirms the pattern: it used T3 100% of turns, resolved only 1/10 (10%), and had the worst yield/$ at 2.0. T3 alone doesn't fix hard tasks — it just costs more.

### 2. budgetflow_segment is the most efficient policy

Yield/$ = 10.0, 3 resolved (tied with bare_t2 for most). Spent $0.24 — less than half of bf_task_level ($0.48) while resolving more tasks. Segment-aware routing (Claim 2) provides visible efficiency lift over task-level (Claim 1) in this small sample.

### 3. budgetflow_same_enterprise_router is stuck

0 resolved, 7 abort, 3 true_fail. The frozen plan's preferred_model choices (mostly T2 for these 10 sympy/flask tasks) plus BudgetFlow's ledger/governor created a strategy that neither resolves tasks nor spends budget. Yield/$ is undefined (0 cost efficiency). This strategy needs postmortem analysis.

### 4. bare_t3 is not a productivity baseline

1/10 resolved. T3's higher per-token cost (t3x3: $5.38/1M output vs T2: $1.12/1M) combined with no routing intelligence makes it a "brute force but fail" baseline. The 6x10 result confirms postmortem Problem 3: bare_t3 being cheaper than bare_t2 was a 6x10 artifact of the old price model.

### 5. Aborts concentrate on hard tasks

sympy-24102 had 5/6 strategies abort (only bare_t3 survived — unrestricted T3 access prevents stagnation but didn't help resolve). sympy-13177 and sympy-13647 each had 4/6 abort. Flask tasks completed well (only 1 abort each). Most aborts are StagnationExit — the agent couldn't produce a valid patch.

### 6. Protocol retry rate is very high (85%)

51/60 pairs had at least one protocol_retry. This means formatting issues are pervasive — likely from the mini-swe-agent v2.3.0 tool protocol, not from the model itself. This inflates costs across all strategies uniformly, so cross-strategy comparisons remain valid. But absolute cost numbers include retry overhead.
